# Controllers

**The link with each controller.** The adapter establishes the Bluetooth link with each DualShock 4. While basic report 0x01 is accepted during initial connection for standard button inputs, enhanced report 0x11 is the sole carrier of motion data and is validated with its CRC-32 before accepting it; audio uplink packets (0x12 to 0x19) and any other reports are discarded so that inconsistent motion data never enters the chain. The link is supervised by watchdogs that retry the connection when the controller moves out of range or the link stalls, and that drop the link when the controller stops responding, so that the controller can reconnect on its own when it returns to range.

**Factory calibration.** During the link, the adapter requests the controller factory calibration through feature report 0x05. That calibration is applied per unit and validated with sanity checks; it also seeds the drift tracker so the initial zero is correct. The process has staged retries: the first attempt is immediate, the following ones are spaced out, and if the controller does not respond the firmware keeps insisting quietly for a long interval before giving up. The result is reported with a blink code on the controller light bar: two green blinks mean calibration applied, three red blinks mean the controller did not respond and four yellow blinks mean the calibration was rejected because it did not pass the sanity checks. This indication is the intended way to diagnose the link without external instruments.

**Synchronization window and two-controller capacity.** The system supports a maximum of two simultaneous DualShock 4 controllers (`USB_OUTPUT_PORTS = 2` in firmware, matching `MAX_FAKE_WIIMOTES = 2` in the console module). The light bar of each controller is dynamically driven by the console's player assignment, following the Nintendo Wii color standard: Player 1 is blue (`#0000FF`), Player 2 is red (`#FF0000`), Player 3 is green (`#00FF00`), and Player 4 is yellow (`#FFFF00`). If real Wiimotes already occupy earlier player slots on the console, connected DualShock 4 controllers automatically adopt the remaining player slots and their respective colors (for example, Player 3 green and Player 4 yellow if real Wiimotes take Player 1 and Player 2).

To link controllers:
- **First controller (Player 1):** With the adapter powered on, press the adapter boot button once. This opens a twenty second synchronization window (`gap_discoverable_control(1)` and timed Bluetooth inquiry scan). Put the first DualShock 4 into pairing mode by holding SHARE and PS until its light bar blinks white. The adapter pairs with it, executes the factory calibration check, and assigns it to Player 1 with a solid blue light bar.
- **Second controller (Player 2):** While Player 1 remains active and connected, press the adapter boot button once again. A new twenty second synchronization window opens without disconnecting or resetting Player 1. Put the second DualShock 4 into pairing mode by holding SHARE and PS until it blinks white. The adapter pairs with it, verifies calibration, and assigns it to Player 2 with a solid red light bar.
- **Automatic radio lock:** As soon as both controller slots are occupied (`conn_count >= 2`), the firmware immediately closes any active sync scan and turns off Bluetooth page scanning (`gap_connectable_control(0)`). This ensures radio bandwidth and airtime are fully dedicated to active gameplay and 16 kHz SBC audio streaming. Any third controller attempting to connect will find the radio unconnectable, blink white, and turn off.
- **Reconnection:** Controllers that have already been paired store their link keys in the adapter's flash memory. When powering on the console or reconnecting after a timeout, pressing the PS button reconnects the controller directly without needing to touch the adapter boot button.
- **Clearing bonds:** A long press of the adapter boot button disconnects all active controllers, clears all stored link keys from flash, resets the audio queues, and opens a fresh twenty second synchronization window.

**Drift control.** The firmware keeps a gyroscope bias tracker with the same logic the MotionPlus uses: while the controller remains still, the bias estimate is updated continuously, and when motion is detected the estimate is frozen so that real motion is never mistaken for drift. The long term drift therefore does not accumulate in the measurement, and the initial bias value starts from the factory calibration. This behaviour is the digital equivalent of what a real remote does internally. The concrete numbers: the gyroscope delivers 16.384 counts per degree per second, which means one count is six hundredths of a degree per second, and the tracker works at that resolution, so a bias of a few counts per second is enough to matter over minutes of play. Freezing the estimate on motion is what keeps a slow drift correction from being mistaken for a slow turn: the tracker distinguishes the two by how much the samples vary, and it only updates when they stand still.

**Units and real rates.** Values are transmitted in the raw units of the controller, without decimation or clipping. The gyroscope delivers 16.384 counts per degree per second over a range of plus or minus 2000 degrees per second, and the accelerometer delivers 8192 counts per g over a range of plus or minus 4 g. The measurement of the link, performed from a computer with a timestamp per report, gave 247.6 reports per second with 246.0 fresh gyroscope samples per second, which means that practically every report carries a new sample and that there is no decimation in the path. That rate is higher than the one of the original remote, whose extension stream is around one hundred reports per second.

**The USB device.** Towards the console, the adapter presents itself as a single wired DualShock 4, and distinguishes up to two controllers by the report identifier as described in the architecture document (Report ID 0x01 for Player 1, 0x02 for Player 2). The DualShock 4 personality is the only one the firmware implements: everything else that other controllers or transports would need has been removed, so the USB device and the Bluetooth stack only contain what this project uses.

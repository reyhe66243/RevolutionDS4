# Architecture

**The division of responsibilities.** REVOLUTIONDS4 is built on two firmwares with clearly separated roles. The adapter, based on the RP2040, is the Bluetooth host of the controllers and the USB device for the console: it links each DualShock 4, retrieves the factory calibration, keeps the gyroscope drift control and builds the reports the console consumes. The IOS module, on the console side, is responsible for emulating the remote with MotionPlus to the game: it answers the radio protocol, maintains the extension registers, delivers the motion data, manages the speaker, the rumble and the LEDs, and administrates the USB link with the adapter.

This division is not a matter of taste. The game believes it is talking to a remote over the console's internal radio, so the emulation of the protocol must necessarily reside in the module on the console side. Everything related to the physical controller and to the processing that happens per sample may reside in the adapter. The rule that orders the design is therefore: the adapter adapts the controller, the module translates the protocol.

**The data path.** In the input direction, the controller delivers its reports over Bluetooth to the adapter, which forwards them over USB to the console; the module converts them into the registers of the emulated remote and the game reads them. In the output direction, the game writes to the speaker, LED and rumble registers of the emulated remote; the module translates them into DualShock 4 reports and sends them over USB to the adapter, which forwards them to the controller over Bluetooth. Every motion sample travels the complete path in a single direction, with no intermediate state accumulation that could desynchronize the two parts.

**The link protocol.** The adapter presents itself to the console as a single USB device shaped like a DualShock 4, and distinguishes the controllers by the report identifier: player one uses identifier 0x01 and player two uses identifier 0x02 in the input direction. In the output direction the console sends reports with identifiers 0x05 and 0x06 respectively, and the audio channel carries the player index in the high nibble of its second byte. The adapter keeps a report counter per player, so each stream preserves its own numbering and its own audio queue. The values travel in the raw units of the controller: the gyroscope with 16.384 counts per degree per second over a range of plus or minus 2000 degrees per second, and the accelerometer with 8192 counts per g over a range of plus or minus 4 g.

**What lives on each side.** The adapter hosts the Bluetooth stack, the link establishment and supervision, the factory calibration retrieval, the drift control, the construction of the per player reports, the audio encoding towards the speaker and the forwarding of rumble and LEDs. The module hosts the complete remote and MotionPlus protocol, the console USB host, the transfer management, the audio queues and the motion gain calibration. The size limits section describes which parts could migrate to the adapter in the future and why.

**Size limit and roadmap.** The planned strategy is to move to the adapter all the work that depends on the controller and that today runs per sample on the console, in this order: the axis mapping together with the orientation logic, the MotionPlus encoding together with the gain calibration, the accelerometer conditioning into remote units and the button and touchpad policy. What must remain on the console is the game protocol, the USB host and the audio queue management, because they depend on the console and not on the controller.
